1What we set out to measure

Five contextual information features, one long-term holdout

Across MLB 2026, the Players, Stats & Research workstream shipped a series of iterative improvements to the event page: team records, broadcast info, starting pitchers, starting lineups, and a team L5 games dropdown. Each was a small, additive context piece. Individually, none were expected to move the needle on their own. The hypothesis was that together, over time, they would compound into a measurable lift in MLB betting behavior: more informed users making more confident decisions, translating into higher engagement and handle.

This holdout is the instrument for that compound question. A persistent 10% of eligible users were withheld from all five features for 81 days (Jul 7 to Sep 25, 2026), while the remaining 90% received each feature as it launched. The analysis is restricted to users who viewed at least one pre-live MLB event page during the window (165,505 holdout / 1,486,436 treatment).

2Executive summary

81-day holdout comparison: 165,505 holdout vs 1,486,436 treatment (pre-live MLB EP viewers)

Top takeaways

  • The compound hypothesis did not materialize: All 7 primary success metrics are statistically null after Bonferroni correction. The five features remain collectively flat.
  • GGR guardrail is clean: Pre-live MLB GGR per user is not statistically different between holdout ($107.04) and treatment ($112.57). The features did not harm revenue.
  • Weekly trends show no divergence: Holdout and treatment track each other closely across 12 weeks of handle, GGR, and bet volume, with no inflection at any feature launch date.
  • Individual features showed real adoption and promising signals: Starting Pitchers lifted bets per user in its own A/B. Lineups had 84.9% modal-to-selection intent. Team L5 had 51.5% open-to-bet CVR. These did not compound into a cumulative commercial effect.
  • Event page retention is flat: Week-over-week return rate to pre-live MLB event pages is within 0.4pp every week. The features do not bring users back at a higher rate.
How to read these numbers. All metrics in this readout are restricted to pre-live MLB event page viewers (166K holdout / 1.49M treatment). Continuous metrics are winsorized at the 99th percentile within each variant. Per-user z-test, Bonferroni-adjusted across 7 primary metrics (adjusted alpha = 0.0071). The test can detect relative effects as small as ~1.5 to 2% on per-user means. n.s. = not statistically significant. Full metric table with p-values and confidence intervals.

7Conclusion

What we learned from 81 days of holdout data

The compound hypothesis was null

The central question was whether five individually-flat contextual information features would compound into a measurable cumulative effect on MLB betting behavior. They did not. Across 7 primary metrics, 3 guardrails, and multiple behavioral cuts, all computed per-user, winsorized, and restricted to pre-live MLB event page viewers, there is no detectable difference between the holdout and treatment groups. The null is robust: it holds across calendar-aligned and days-since-bucketing analyses, across value segments, and across the full 81-day window.

The features are not without value

Individual feature tests showed real adoption and genuine user intent. Starting Pitchers lifted Bets/User in its own A/B. Starting Lineups had 84.9% modal-to-selection conversion. Team L5 Games had 51.5% open-to-bet conversion among adopters. These signals are real: they show users engaging with contextual information and acting on it. What they don't do is shift the population-level commercial metrics. The features make the event page more informative without making it more commercially productive.

Experiment design considerations

  • Team L5 Games was added to the holdout after its own A/B (Jun 20 to 27). Holdout users may have had brief exposure before suppression, a small contamination window that biases toward null.
  • Team L5 Games was rolled back due to a bug. The feature was turned off in late August after causing a bug, removing one of the five features from the treatment experience for the last month of the window. Treatment users had only four active features for roughly a third of the analysis period.
  • Team Records had intermittent display issues during its test window. The feature appeared and disappeared, so treatment exposure was inconsistent. Analysis was anchored to a clean sub-window (Jul 31 to Aug 9) rather than the full run.
  • NFL season overlap after Sep 7 introduced a reactivation wave into both arms. The holdout test plan recommended wrapping before NFL kickoff; this analysis runs 3 weeks past it. The late-window dilution from NFL-driven users (who visit for football, not MLB) works against detecting an MLB-specific effect.

Why did this not work the way we expected?

  • The features may be too small individually to compound: Each feature is a passive information display (a record, a name, a network). None changes the betting interaction itself. Users notice them (adoption is real) but their decisions don't change measurably.
  • Context may reduce friction without increasing volume: Users who already bet on MLB get slightly better-informed, but they were going to bet anyway. The features solve a research problem, not a conversion problem.
  • The window included MLB's declining engagement arc: Jul through Sep is the second half of the MLB season. If compounding requires users to build a research habit, the declining seasonal interest works against that.

Next steps and off-season hypotheses

  • Test features that change the interaction, not just the information: Quick-bet surfaces on stats (e.g. "bet the over on this pitcher's strikeouts" directly from the pitcher card) might convert the engagement that contextual stats create.
  • Run the holdout across a full sport lifecycle for 2027 NFL or NBA: A holdout from season opener through the end of the regular season gives the compound hypothesis its best shot, with the highest-engagement users in the window from day one.

Assumptions to revisit

  • The "give a mouse a cookie" effect: The current features may give users just enough context to become more curious about their bet, but not enough to satisfy that curiosity in-app. If users engage with a starting pitcher's ERA and then leave the app to research further, the features could be driving external research rather than replacing it. Future features should test whether closing the loop in-app (deeper stats, player comparisons, historical matchups) converts that curiosity into action.
  • Does more data actually make users more confident or decisive? The core assumption behind contextual stats is that information leads to confidence and confidence leads to betting. But some users may see data that causes them to second-guess their instinct, either deciding not to bet or to bet less. If the features occasionally talk users out of a bet, the net commercial effect could be zero even with real engagement. Worth measuring directly: do users who interact with these features place bets at the same rate as those who don't?

9Methodology

Cohort construction, data sources, statistical approach

Flag

Cohort
Materialized at sandbox.sportsbook_commercial_product_analytics.holdout_contextual_stats_cohort. Treatment: 3,847,658 users (~90%). Holdout: 427,745 users (~10%). Users bucketed on a rolling basis as eligible.
Window
Jul 7 to Sep 25, 2026 (81 days). Team Records launched Jul 7; analysis starts there. Features already rolled out for playoffs. This is a retrospective analysis.
Gated flags
atlutd_l5_coupon_stats, event_sdui_team_records_header, event_sdui_broadcast_header, event_sdui_header_starting_pitchers, event_sdui_header_starting_lineups
Stat test
Two-sided z-test on the Welch (unequal-variance) statistic. Analytical confidence intervals. Bonferroni correction across 7 primary metrics (adjusted alpha = 0.0071). Guardrails and behavioral metrics reported at α = 0.05 without family-wise correction.
Winsorization
All continuous metrics winsorized at the 99th percentile within each variant before testing. Harsher than the standard 99.5th due to the ~9:1 size asymmetry (428K holdout vs 3.85M treatment).
Bet data
core_views.sportsbook.bet_legs for handle, GGR, bets, selections, player prop legs. core_views.sportsbook.bet_legs_eggr for eGGR. Pre-live MLB filtered by is_event_in_play = FALSE and leg_competition_name_reporting LIKE '%mlb%'. Player props filtered by market name patterns.
Amplitude data
Event Page metrics (EP bets, EP handle, EP bet share) from amplitude_sportsbook_bet via Selection Success events with Selection Page Name Source LIKE '%event%'. Sessions, CVR, and ATBS from amplitude_sportsbook_page_view joined to bet events. All joined to cohort via user_id.
Base filters
All metrics: is_test_account = FALSE, is_clubhouse_account = FALSE. Pre-live MLB: In Play = false, Competition = mlb. ATBS uses Add Selection to Betslip, not Selection Success.
